Read for where the ban's own scope moved between Part A and Part B, and who ended up carrying the cost of replacement once it landed. ---
LayerReadingWhen
Object of the rule Relational. Five named manufacturers (Huawei, ZTE, Hytera, Hikvision, Dahua), and whether an entity uses their equipment anywhere in its operations, not only in the federal contract itself. named in the 2018 statute
Design Layer Boundary redraw. Part A, already in force, had banned agencies from buying the equipment directly. Part B extended the ban to any contractor that merely uses it anywhere in its business, regardless of any connection to the federal work. Numeric trigger: the statute fixed the effective date two years after enactment, independent of any contractor's conduct. statute enacted Aug 2018, Part B dated for Aug 2020
Cost Layer See matrix below. Filled as of July 2020, before the effective date. as of Jul 2020
Convergence Reallocation. The named manufacturers had little left to lose; the outcome landed on the domestic contractors and subcontractors with the least ability to replace equipment on short notice. after Aug 13, 2020

Timeline

Aug 13, 2018Section 889 enacted as part of the FY2019 NDAA, with Part A and Part B both written into the statute at different fixed effective dates.
Aug 13, 2019Part A takes effect: agencies may not directly procure covered equipment.
Jul 14, 2020The FAR Council publishes the Part B interim rule, citing pandemic-related delay and the complexity of the analysis required; it skips the normal 60-day comment period to meet the statute's own deadline.
Aug 13, 2020Part B takes effect on schedule. Agencies may not contract with, extend, or renew contracts with any entity using covered equipment anywhere in its operations.
Through Aug 13, 2022Any waivers granted under the interim rule run out; contractors are expected to have phased out covered equipment by that date.

Who held what, and where it landed
