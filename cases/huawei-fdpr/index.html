Convergence, read twice

PointReading
While the license heldReallocation. Huawei's low substitution was masked by stockpiling; the exposure itself sat with TSMC (sustained revenue) and U.S. chipmakers (frozen, license-pending revenue), not with Huawei.
Once it lapsedDirect hit. TSMC substituted its customer base inside the same quarter; the outcome converged on the one actor with no substitution available: Huawei.

Timeline

May 16, 2019Huawei and affiliates added to the Entity List.
May 2019 to Mar 2020A temporary general license, renewed four times on a fixed 90-day cycle, keeps existing U.S. transactions running. Each renewal is calendar-triggered, not conditioned on Huawei's conduct.
May 15, 2020BIS expands the Foreign-Produced Direct Product Rule to reach foreign-made chips built with U.S.-origin equipment or technology, with a wind-down window for orders already in production.
Aug 13 to 17, 2020The general license lapses without further renewal; BIS adds further Huawei affiliates and confirms the expanded rule.
Sept 14 to 15, 2020TSMC's wind-down window closes. No further shipments to Huawei or HiSilicon without a license.

Who held what, and where it landed

ActorAuthority heldWhat happened instead
Commerce (BIS)Set a fixed wind-down date, independent of any actor's behavior.The rule fired exactly on schedule; no discretion was exercised at the deadline.
HuaweiSource chips through TSMC while the license ran.Stockpiled ahead of the deadline; production of TSMC-made Kirin chips ended once the window closed.
TSMCFulfil existing orders through the wind-down window.Confirmed it stopped accepting new Huawei orders mid-May 2020 and shipped nothing after Sept 14; replaced the lost revenue with other customers inside the same quarter.
U.S. chipmakers (Qualcomm, Intel, Micron)Apply for individual licenses to continue limited sales.Applications sat pending through the license period; some received narrower licenses after the deadline.
U.S. rural carriersContinue servicing existing Huawei-built networks.Protected by the general license while it ran; later covered by separate federal replacement funding, outside this rule.
