APPLIED EXAMPLE

Applied Example

Two real rules, read through Stage 1 and 2 independently, then together through Stage 3, then turned into a firm-level matrix at Stage 4. The firm in Section D is hypothetical; the two rules are not.

Section A: BIS Affiliates Rule

StageReading
Object (Stage 1)Relational: an ownership tie to a party already on the Entity List, MEU List, or SDN List. The rule does not name a new party directly; it names a relationship to one already named.
Design (Stage 2)Redraws the line. Numeric trigger: 50 percent or more aggregate ownership by one or more listed parties.
Scope traceAuthorizing text: ECRA 2018, EAR Part 744. Stated aim: closing an evasion channel where listed parties operate through affiliates. Operative reach: a new freestanding restriction on any entity meeting the ownership threshold, not limited to entities already reviewed. Gap: a new class of party, the affiliate itself, not previously subject to a listing determination.
Sept 29, 2025Rule takes effect. A 60-day temporary general license issued alongside it authorizes transactions with certain non-listed affiliates through Nov 28, 2025.

Nov 9, 2026Scheduled lift, absent further action.

Section B: China Rare Earth Controls

StageReading
Object (Stage 1)Two independent attribute-based triggers: Chinese-origin content share of 0.1 percent or more, and Chinese-origin technology used in production, regardless of transit route.
Design (Stage 2)Redraws the line. Numeric trigger on content share; conduct language in Announcement No. 62 on technology origin.
Scope traceAuthorizing text: China's Export Control Law, 2020. Stated aim: national security and non-proliferation. Operative reach: a foreign-to-foreign transaction that never touches Chinese territory. Gap: China's first extraterritorial export-control rule.
Oct 9, 2025Announcements 55 through 58 and 61 through 62 take effect.
Nov 7, 2025Suspended, as part of the same bilateral trade arrangement following the Trump-Xi Busan meeting.
Nov 10, 2026Scheduled lift, absent further action.

Section C: Stage 3, cross-reading applied

Object comparison: the BIS rule's object is person-type, an ownership tie. The China rule's object is thing-type, a material and technology makeup. Different object types route to different parts of a firm by default: ownership diligence to legal, content and technology origin to supply-chain and technical teams. Neither team sees the whole picture unless the two readings are put side by side.

Design comparison: both rules independently moved from a territorial basis to a nexus-based one. Neither references the other. A third-country firm can be caught by both at once without either regulator coordinating on that outcome.

Section D: Stage 4, Cost Layer applied

Firm X: a Korea-headquartered rare-earth magnet manufacturer. A joint-venture partner is 50 percent or more owned by an Entity-Listed party, triggering the BIS rule. The magnets contain roughly 2 to 4 percent Chinese-origin heavy rare earths, triggering the China rule. The two facts are independent of each other; neither caused the other.

VariableReading, informed by Section C
Distance from objectDirect on both: a JV partner tie for BIS, direct material content for the China rule.
Revenue share exposedFirm-specific; both rules touch the same product line.
Substitution availableA non-tied, non-Chinese heavy-rare-earth supplier would resolve both exposures at once, since the two triggers meet in the same supply chain.
Prior positioningOwnership-chain diligence and a bill-of-materials review can run in parallel, since they sit in different departments by the Section C object comparison.

BIS rule lifts on schedule Both exposures close on their stated dates; the JV and supplier questions remain worth resolving regardless, since either rule can be reinstated. BIS exposure closes; China exposure continues, isolated to the supply-chain track.
BIS rule extended China exposure closes; BIS exposure continues, isolated to the ownership track. Both remain open; the supplier switch carries the most weight of any single action available.

Priority order: qualify an alternate heavy-rare-earth supplier; run ownership-chain diligence and a bill-of-materials review in parallel; decide the sequencing of disclosure to BIS and to MOFCOM before either filing is made.
